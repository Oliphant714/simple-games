# Magic School Story Outline

## Tone Ideas

The tone should be slightly mysterious, calm, and emotional rather than heavily action-based.  It should feel like a light school drama with a gentle slice-of-life feel.  It should gradually bleed into a strange/serious story through a slow-burn mystery.  Despite the psychological themes of the secret of the Velvet Arcanum, it shouldn't feel like a horror or pyschological terror.  It should be intriguing, investing, and still possible to let the player sleep at night.

## Core Premise

The game follows a main character attending a School of Magic, The Velvet Arcanum, made up of seven very different colleges. The player can choose whether the main character is male or female, then choose a college and, based on the spells they chooce, one of that college's two tracks, which work like the school's version of a BA or BS. Each college focuses on a different field of study, and its magic is specialized for classwork, social situations, part-time work, and story events.

The game is designed to be simple and readable. Most of the gameplay happens in a daily loop:

- Wake up in the dorm
- Choose an activity
- Attend class
- Spend time with NPCs or work a part-time job
- Return to the dorm at night
- Rest and begin the next day

The main story should feel slightly unclear at first. As the player continues, they notice small inconsistencies in how teachers and students react to certain places, topics, and events. These clues eventually lead to the uncovering of a much larger secret.  A careful player will notice that occasionally the 'date' will jump forward in ways that aren't normal; Monday will move to Thursday, Tuesday will be the 12th and Wednesday will be the 22nd, etc.

## Seven Colleges

Each college should feel like a different world. Their students should dress differently, speak differently, and solve problems differently. The magic should follow the college's discipline, not a basic elemental theme.

### 1. College of Civic Law

- Focus: contracts, testimony, dispute resolution, magical procedure
- Track A: Pre-Law and Tribunal Practice
- Track B: Enforcement and Public Statutes
- Magic style: loophole creations, truth pressure, precedent seals, courtroom wards
- Typical use: forcing honest answers, securing agreements, unlocking legal archives, stopping unlawful magic

### 2. College of Vital Sciences

- Focus: medicine, anatomy, healing, research
- Track A: Clinical Care and Diagnosis
- Track B: Surgical and Regenerative Arts
- Magic style: pulse reading, cleansing, tissue knitting, pain suppression
- Typical use: healing injuries, identifying poisons, treating magical illness, stabilizing patients after strange events

### 3. College of Computational Arts

- Focus: computers, logic systems, codes, synthetic mana
- Track A: Software and Spell Systems
- Track B: Networks and Arcane Security
- Magic style: cipher work, pattern tracing, predictive routines, machine-soul interfacing
- Typical use: repairing magical devices, decoding messages, bypassing security, tracing digital ghosts in campus systems

### 4. College of Built Worlds

- Focus: construction, architecture, infrastructure, repair
- Track A: Structural Design and Surveying
- Track B: Construction Management and Materials
- Magic style: reinforcement, load reading, spatial shaping, rapid assembly
- Typical use: fixing dorms, altering floor plans, stabilizing dangerous rooms, creating temporary barriers or platforms

### 5. College of Thought and Ethics

- Focus: philosophy, logic, metaphysics, theory
- Track A: Logic and Argument
- Track B: Metaphysics and Applied Theory
- Magic style: concept shaping, debate pressure, paradox handling, perception testing
- Typical use: exposing contradictions, resisting mental influence, interpreting obscure magical laws, unlocking symbolic puzzles

### 6. College of Civic Commerce

- Focus: economics, management, trade, logistics, negotiation
- Track A: Accounting and Administration
- Track B: Supply Chains and Negotiation
- Magic style: valuation, transport marks, resource tracking, contract favors
- Typical use: running a part-time job, improving campus services, moving goods, bargaining for rare supplies, tracking missing items

### 7. College of Arts and Influence

- Focus: performance, media, public image, communication
- Track A: Performance and Direction
- Track B: Journalism and Public Messaging
- Magic style: attention shaping, emotional resonance, illusion of presence, message amplification
- Typical use: calming crowds, gathering gossip, improving relationships, uncovering hidden motives, creating social leverage

## Main Character Setup

The player should be able to choose:

- Male or female protagonist
- Name
- Starting college
- Starting track within that college
- Basic personality flavor, if desired, but not a complex class system
- Part-time job if they decide to look for/take one

The protagonist should feel flexible enough that the player can imagine their own version of the character.

## Daily Loop

The game should follow a simple repeated schedule.

### Morning

- Wake up in the dorm
- Check mood, energy, and any relationship messages
- Choose breakfast, study, social time, or errands

### Midday

- Attend class
- Learn one spell, gain one clue to the overall mystery, or nothing depending on the day and the actions chosen
- Resolve a small event or conversation

### Afternoon

- Visit NPCs
- Study in the library
- Take a part-time job
- Practice spells
- Explore campus

### Evening

- Return to the dorm
- Review the day
- Rest to recover energy
- Advance to the next day

The loop should be straightforward, with enough choices to feel alive but not so many systems that it becomes complicated.

## Relationship System

Relationships should be easy to understand and manage.

Suggested system:

- Each important NPC has a relationship score
- Choices and conversations increase or decrease that score
- Some scenes unlock only at certain relationship thresholds
- A few NPCs can become close friends, rivals, or romantic options if desired

NPCs should react differently based on:

- The player’s college
- The spells they know
- Their current relationship score
- Story flags from earlier days

This keeps the social side meaningful without adding too much complexity.

## Spell Progression

Each college should give the player a different list of field-specific spells that can be used in various situations.

Suggested structure:

- Basic spell learned early
- Track-specific utility spell
- Utility spell learned later
- Social or exploration spell
- Advanced spell tied to the mystery or late-game story

Spells should be useful in more than one context. For example:

- A law spell might enforce a promise, challenge a false statement, or seal a document
- A medicine spell might diagnose a condition, stabilize an ally, or reveal tampering
- A computer spell might trace a signal, restore corrupted data, or unlock a protected system
- A construction spell might brace a wall, reshape a room, or expose hidden architecture

The player should feel like their college and track choice matters, while still being able to complete the main story from any route.

## The Mystery Structure

The story should begin as a normal academy life game, but small details slowly become wrong.

Examples of inconsistencies:

- A teacher remembers an event differently from a student
- A hallway appears in one scene but not another
- Two NPCs describe the same spell in contradictory ways
- A classroom seems to reset overnight
- A magical law is treated as obvious by one character and impossible by another

These clues should be subtle early on, then become harder to ignore as the player progresses.

## The Secret of The Velvet Arcanum

- The Hook: You notice that every semester, the faculty quietly alters the academic calendar, shaving off a few days here, a week there, until entire months seem to be missing from the historical archives. When you compare your class notes with an alumni sibling from three years prior, you realize you are studying the exact same lectures for semesters that supposedly never happened. Worse, students who ask too many questions about the missing time don't get expelled—they are quietly reassigned to a low-profile maintenance track called "Chronological Upkeep," and they return looking hollow, aged, and obsessively focused on keeping schedules.

- The Secret: The school is not just a campus; it is a heavy temporal anchor holding back a localized reality collapse. Every few years, the timeline fractures under the weight of accumulated magical research and outside dimensional pressure. To prevent the entire town and university from sliding backward into a permanent chronological void, the faculty must periodically "prune" time—wiping entire weeks, erasing graduating cohorts who witnessed the fracture, and scrubbing memory to keep the universe's math balanced. The strict rules and bizarrely specific curfews aren’t arbitrary school discipline; they are the precise behavioral synchronization required to keep the campus from slipping out of phase with reality altogether.

### Possible Endings
- The Synchronized Collapse (The Truth Wins)
    The Choice: Refuse to let another cohort be quietly erased. You lead a coordinated student movement to breach the administration's primary clockwork vault and intentionally shatter the temporal anchors during the middle of the school year.

    The Outcome: The illusion fails completely. The campus snaps violently back into its true historical timeline, revealing decades of stacked memory fragments, ghostly echoes of erased students, and the terrifying expanse of the void pressing against the gates. While the school is forced to shut down permanently and the faculty face a reckoning, the survivors finally wake up to reality, carrying an unvarnished, un-wiped history into the outside world.

- The Burden Passed (The New Wardens)
    The Choice: Realize that the math doesn't lie—if the temporal anchors drop, everyone dies. Instead of fighting the system, you use your investigation to uncover the flaws in the current pruning algorithm, earning the respect of the surviving faculty.

    The Outcome: You and your inner circle are initiated into the inner circle of Chronological Upkeep. You step into the roles of the teachers you once feared, learning how to carefully snip away inconvenient timelines and wipe incoming freshman memories to keep the campus safe. You survive, graduate with honors, and become part of the machine—perpetuating the protective lie to save the future.

- The Anchor's Rebellion (The Third Path)
    The Choice: Reject both total exposure (suicide for the campus) and silent compliance (sacrifice of the innocent). Using rare temporal spells and the clues left behind by the erased students before you, you reverse-engineer the school's anchors to draw stabilization energy from the external threat itself rather than human memory.

    The Outcome: You successfully rewrite the university’s foundational code. The campus no longer needs to erase semesters or wipe student minds to stay anchored in reality. The missing cohorts and lost memories don't return, but the cycle of institutional gaslighting is permanently broken, turning the school from a desperate survival bunker into an open, honest academy facing the cosmos on its own terms.

## Simple Gameplay Priorities

To keep the game easy to build:

- Use a small number of stats
- Keep spell effects straightforward
- Make daily choices short and readable
- Reuse locations often
- Let story flags and relationships carry most of the complexity

Suggested core stats:

- Energy
- Magic proficiency
- Relationship scores
- College reputation
- Mystery awareness

## Important NPCs

A basic list of the first main NPCs the character should meet and regularly interact with

- Alex Cartwright, an elf the same gender and college as the MC, easy going and fun classmate, tries to help the MC through the adventure.

- Britches Elinbriin, looks like a male fairy though his true form is a massive dragon, President of the Velvet Arcanum, weilds magic unlike any of the other schools (he has lived long enough to earn degrees from each of them and has started his own secret club that studies new magics, the burgeouning College of Physics and Reality), is the MC's greates supporter and always believes in them from a distance

- Lily Arcabloom, a short female human, actively interested in the MC (romantically if male, platonically if female), studies at the College of Vital Sciences.

- Darian Wyldman, a tall and brawny male human from the College of Built Worlds, actively interested in the MC (romantically if female, platonically if male).

- Moei, the male aaracockran (eagle person) dean of the College of Civic Law, not the villian but feels like it, uses his law magic to enact order (the only beings who seem capable of resisting his magic are the school President and, when their skills increase enough, the MC), genuinely enjoys teaching but doesn't enjoy lawlessness of any kind

- Anacor Silence, a female and incredibly attractive serpentian (snake person) from the College of Art and Influence, the MC's rival, a queen-bee personality who uses her magic to manipulate people into liking her, ruthless as the Bible's Jezebel and smart as the Bible's Solomon

- Felix Fergerben Flip the Fifth, a male human nerd from the College of Computational Arts, both the driving force and the hidden villian of the story, Felix is resentful at the stereotype of his college and is trying to uncover the secret of the Velvet Arcanum so he can use it for himself, a sly but spineless character, he plays the part of the 'damsel in distress' so the MC will do what he needs them to do

- Clark Wayne, the male orc boss at the MC's job, while incredibly friendly and knowledgeable to all the town gossip, he runs a tight ship and pays a fair wage, he supports the academic life but is constantly interested in the MC's romantic life as well

## Starting Locations

A list of the basic locations the MC will visit.  Each location will (eventually) have a few backdrops depending on the event running.  The NPCs and events available in each location will shift depending on the time of day, day of the week, and various story flags.

- The Dorm Room
    A basic room with whatever decorations the MC has purchased.  Contains a mirror, bed, and desk at the beginning.  The mirror allows the MC to check their stats and relationships through a "Mirror, Mirror on the Wall" type of deal.  The bed is where they rest through the night or end a day early by going to bed.  The desk is available for extra study which can grant magic bonuses and make it easier to learn spells.

- The Classroom
    An ordinary classroom with desks and chairs.  The MC will come here every weekday and learn a little something.  Sometimes, they will get the option to learn new spells or receive clues to the main story here.  Each day, the MC will get the chance to interact with one student in class and affect their relationship with them.

- The Library
    An optional location to go before or after class.  Here the MC can choose to study additional magics that aren't part of their regular major.  The President Britches Elinbriin often spends time here and can interact with the MC.  If the MC learns enough varied spells here, the President Britches Elinbriin might offer for the MC to join his secret club.

- Job Location
    The only variable location, it is determined by the player's choice of part-time job.  A few things are consistent throughout all the jobs however.  When the player chooses to go to work, it takes up their entire evening and they return to bed at the end.  Sometimes events will occur here that will lead to opened options at the school to find more clues.  Rarely will events here affect relationships with anyone other than the boss, Clark Wayne.

- Courtyard
    An in-between location, whenever the MC leaves their dorm to go anywhere, there is a chance of an encounter happening here.  They might run into a friend or have a quick showdown with a rival.  Events here will always affect relationships and can sometimes leave clues to the overall secret.

## Part-time Jobs

The MC will have the choice to walk around town, peruse the stores, and potentially pick up a part-time job.  This is the list of shops that are offering positions.  Each position has a Gig (the basic information about the job that the player learns when choosing their part-time job), Daily Duties (randomly chosen basic events that play out when the MC goes to work), and Student Perks (bonuses the MC can add to their litanny of abilities and options).

- Delivery Courier for Mercury & Co. Fast Freight
    The Gig: Delivering everything from urgent takeout orders to forgotten textbooks across town using standard or slightly modified transportation.

    Daily Duties: Navigating cobbled streets on a broomstick, enchanted bicycle, or rolling skateboard; dodging rogue weather anomalies spawned by local meteorology students; and figuring out how to deliver a hot bowl of dragon-pepper ramen to a 4th-floor walk-up without spilling a drop.

    Student Perk: Excellent tips from desperate students cramming through midnight study sessions, plus you get to know every hidden shortcut and back alley in town.

- Barista & Night-Shift Bouncer at The Gremlin’s Mug
    The Gig: Working the late shift at the town's most popular 24-hour diner and study haunt, frequented by locals and night-owl academics alike.

    Daily Duties: Flipping pancakes on a griddle powered by a captured hearth-spirit, brewing memory-boosting herbal teas, and gently (or forcefully) tossing out rowdy apprentices who try to duel over table space after one too many mugs of sparkling mead.

    Student Perk: Free post-shift breakfast platters and a front-row seat to all the local town gossip.

- Inventory Sorter & Appraiser at Curio & Co. Pawnshop
    The Gig: Working behind the counter of a dusty secondhand shop that buys and sells mystery boxes, estate sale leftovers, and cast-off magical trinkets from adventurers and alumni.

    Daily Duties: Dusting antique mirrors that occasionally talk back, testing second-hand wands for residual charges, sorting piles of unlabeled keys, and determining whether a glowing brass figurine is a cursed artifact or just a cheap paperweight.

    Student Perk: First pick of cool vintage gear, textbooks, and room decor before it hits the sales floor, usually at a heavy employee discount.

- Assistant Groomer at The Gilded Griffin Pet Spa
    The Gig: A neighborhood grooming parlor specializing in exotic companions, familiars, and working animals belonging to town residents and wealthy commuters.

    Daily Duties: Washing mud off griffins' tail feathers, detangling the fur of multi-tailed foxes, filing down the horns of miniature drakes, and calming nervous hellhound puppies during thunderous spring storms.

    Student Perk: Hands-on experience with magical creature care outside of rigid academic settings, plus generous holiday bonuses from appreciative pet owners.

- Line Cook at The Boiler & Basil, a Fusion Pizzeria
    The Gig: Slashing prep times and tossing pies at a popular local pizza joint known for infusing traditional wood-fired cooking with low-level elemental magic.

    Daily Duties: Stoking the brick ovens using localized fire-elemental coals, kneading dough at lightning speed, and managing the magical refrigeration units that keep perishable ingredients fresh indefinitely.

    Student Perk: Free custom pizzas at the end of every shift and a flexible schedule built around midterms and project deadlines.
